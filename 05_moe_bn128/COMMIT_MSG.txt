perf(sparse-moe): let the grouped prefill job width follow the chunk

The routed grouped GEMMs run one job per expert column tile, and the tile was 64 columns wide at
every chunk width. Each job re-reads its expert's entire weight slab, and at top-8 of 256 the
average column run per expert is tokens/32, so a 64-wide job covers a 2048-token slice exactly and
re-reads every slab twice at 4096 and four times at 8192. The weight traffic of the routed MoE is a
function of the job width and the slice width together, and only one of the two followed the caller.

Both routed GEMMs gain a 128-wide instantiation and the plan picks it from the token count.
sparse_moe_prefill_qx_down_kernel needed generalising to reach it: its accumulator, its B fragments
and its epilogue were written for exactly one eight-column group per warp, the way
sparse_moe_prefill_q4_gate_up_kernel already was not.

A 128-wide tile doubles Bs and three blocks per SM stop fitting, so one expression owns both the
launch bound and the grid and they cannot drift apart. The numbers under it are measured rather than
assumed: the device reports sharedMemPerMultiprocessor 102400 and reservedSharedMemPerBlock 1024,
and the wide instantiations take 50176, 48128 and 49152 bytes, which is 100352 for two blocks of the
gate/up and 150528 for three. Registers hold two blocks with two to spare, 126 x 256 x 2 = 64512
against 65536, and nothing spills.

The nineteen existing instantiations are untouched in registers, shared, local and stack:
prefill_blocks_per_sm(64) is the 3 the literal used to be, and the WarpNT generalisation collapses
to the old shape where WarpNT is 1.

Nothing about the workspace moves. sparse_moe_prefill_workspace_bytes is unchanged at every width,
the arena sequence is untouched, and the engine's reported peak is the same on both arms.

Job width divides the column range and changes no reduction: each job accumulates over the hidden
dimension for its own columns, and a wider job carries more columns rather than reordering a sum.
Greedy output is byte-identical to master.
